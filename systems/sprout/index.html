SproutPixel-prop kit

A UI kit in which every control is a small physical prop: buttons are baked pieces, sliders are planks, headings are painted signs. Everything is drawn on a 3px pixel grid, and there is not one smooth curve in it.

Two things to hold on to. Everything is a whole number of units - size, corner, edge, bevel, gap and every step of motion is an integer multiple of --px, so the kit rescales by changing one value. And pieces are objects, not paint: at night the ground goes dark but a piece keeps its baked colour, dimmed as if lamplit. It never inverts.

Read this first

  1. These values are the system. Every token below is declared in css/sprout-core.css. Use them; do not invent parallel ones.
  2. Search for an existing piece before building a second one. A new control is almost always an existing shell, face and bevel with a different label.
  3. Whole units only. Every size is calc(n * var(--px)) with a whole n. No raw hex, no half units, no one-off pixel sizes.
  4. No border-radius, anywhere. Corners are clip-path staircases. A single rounded corner breaks the illusion for the whole page.
  5. Token names are roles. --piece is the face of a control, not "a beige". Read what a token is for before reaching for it.
  6. Motion is steps() only. Nothing eases, fades or blurs. If it moves, it jumps.

Why two inks

Text on a piece and text on the ground look like one colour by day, and the kit could have used one token for both. It does not, because at night they have to part ways: the ground goes dark and needs a light ink, while the pieces stay lamplit and keep a dark one. --ink and --ground-ink are the same idea on two different surfaces, and naming them separately is what lets night exist without a single exception in the component CSS.

The same reasoning split --alt from --ink: by day both are one cocoa, but a track has to clear 3:1 against a dark board and a lamplit sheet at once, and a text ink does not.

Why the split is by length

The pixel face is paint on a prop. At label length it reads as something lettered onto the object; stretched across a paragraph it becomes tiring, because every glyph is a block of squares. So the rule is decided by length, not by importance: a label, a title, a caption goes pixel; anything a reader has to follow across more than a line goes to the body face, however important it is.

Display sizes are fixed px and run 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, never a rem: a painted label is part of the piece it sits on, and the piece is sized in units, not by the reader's text setting.

Why one unit

Because it makes the whole kit scale as a single object. Set --px: 6px on any wrapper and the geometry inside doubles - corners, edges, bevels, gaps and sprites together - and still lands on the grid. Only the painted labels hold their size, since type is set in fixed pixels. A kit with a free-form spacing scale cannot do that: its 10px gap and its 3px edge would drift out of step the moment either was scaled. The Form section below shows the same button at 1x and 2x.

Section 04

Form

The silhouette is three layers. An outer layer in the edge colour, clipped to a staircase and padded by one unit; a face inside it, clipped to the same staircase; and a one-unit bevel on the face - light above, dark below. Press a piece and the bevel goes while the label drops exactly one unit.

Why layers and not borders

A CSS border is drawn along the element's box and then cut by the clip-path, so at every stepped corner the border is sliced off and the outline opens. An outer layer clipped to the same staircase has no box edge to lose: the edge is simply the one unit of outer colour left showing around the face, and it follows every step. The same reason puts the focus ring on an unclipped wrapper - a clip-path would cut the ring away too.

Section 05

Motion

Toy motion. Things jump between whole positions and never pass through the space between them. Flip the toggle and watch the knob hop; the tendrils on every sign sway one unit each way.

reducedprefers-reduced-motionThe sway stops and the knob jumps without its two steps. State changes stay instant, so nothing is lost.

Why steps and not easing

An eased transition renders every in-between position, and on a kit drawn in 3px squares the in-betweens land between squares - for a frame or two the art is blurred. steps() only ever shows positions on the grid, so the illusion holds while things move. The sway is written as explicit holds rather than alternate for a practical reason: a one-step timing played in reverse never leaves its first frame, and an alternating sway silently stands still.

Screen

One optional surface treatment: a furrow field. The board resolves into whole cells in mown stripes, with a few tufts flicking on a slow tick. Move across the right-hand panel and rings of cells lift in a pixel diamond around the pointer, travelling outward one whole cell per tick.

Why it cannot blend

The kit refuses gradients, and the shader makes that refusal structural rather than a matter of care. Each cell picks one of five token colours and never mixes two: there is no fractional weight anywhere in it. Space is quantised to the cell and time to the tick, so the field moves the way the rest of the kit moves - in steps. It also only redraws when a whole step changes: a new tick, a new cell under the pointer, a ring gained or lost.

Enhancement only. The canvas is transparent until its first frame, so a machine without WebGL2 sees the static stripes on the left and loses nothing. It stops when scrolled off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion it draws one settled frame and never starts a loop.

Where it belongs: an empty state, a title screen, the frame around a hero sign - a place with nothing to read. Not behind body text or controls: a moving checker under a label costs the contrast the Colour section measured, and a ripple under a button reads as the button doing something it is not.

Why every control is three spans deep

Shell, face, content. The shell carries the edge, the face carries the bevel, and the content is what moves when the piece is pressed - so the outline stays put while the label drops a unit, the way a real key would. Collapse the layers into one element and one of those three jobs has nowhere to live. The real input is always present and only visually hidden, so every toggle, check and seed dot is keyboard and screen-reader native.

Section 08

Motifs

What makes the kit recognisable at a glance, before a single colour is read. Two ways of laying pixels - stacked box-shadows and integer SVG rects - and a handful of shapes that recur everywhere.

Staircase corner. Two steps, never a curve.

Sign

Painted sign. Accent face, glint rim, accent-lo edge and shade, two tendrils.

Tip

Stepped tail. One cell plus stacked box-shadow pixels, shown at 4x.

Rect-art sprite. Integer rects, crispEdges, 16 cells.

Seed dot. Two-tone: outline in color, meat in --seed-meat.

Segment dash. Filled or outlined, nothing in between.

Why these, and not the palette

Recolour the kit and it is still recognisable, because the staircase, the one-unit bevel and the rect-art are doing the identifying. That is also why night was possible without redrawing anything: every motif is geometry plus role tokens, and the geometry does not care which theme it is standing in. Before adding a motif, check that it is made of whole units and at most one bevel.

Why it says nothing

Item one, item two, an amount, confirm. Those words were chosen because they imply nothing about what the kit is for. The same arrangement is a settings sheet, an order form, a level select or a file picker, and a reader in any of those fields should be able to lift it whole.

Section 10

Rules

The refusals matter more than the permissions.

Do

  • Build every size as whole units of --px.
  • Draw corners with the staircase clip, and edges as an outer layer.
  • Give depth with a one-unit bevel, and take it away when pressed.
  • Use flat fills and chunky rect-art with crispEdges.
  • Put small text on the ground in --ground-ink, and on a piece in --ink.
  • Check both themes with the Day / night button in the masthead.

Do not

  • Use border-radius, or any smooth curve.
  • Add a gradient, a blur, or a shadow with any blur radius.
  • Ease anything. Motion is steps() only.
  • Set small text in --piece-edge, or small glint text on the accent.
  • Invert a piece for night. The ground goes dark; the piece is lamplit.
  • Run the furrow field behind text or controls.
  • Size the pixel face in rem or in-between steps. It runs 10, 15, 20, 30, 40.
