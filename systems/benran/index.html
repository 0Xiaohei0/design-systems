Design system - stationery of emptiness

Benran - "as it is, of itself" - is a stationery language built from emptiness. Every surface begins as bare paper, and the work is deciding how little may enter: small ink objects resting on one hairline horizon, words standing upright as on a scroll, colour spent one seal at a time.

Two things to hold on to. Content occupies about a tenth of any surface - when a composition feels thin, remove something rather than enlarge what is left. And nothing floats: every object sits on a horizon, a trail or a ripple.

Benran Stationery system No. 01 本然

00Read this first

  1. These values are the system. Every token on this page exists in css/benran-core.css; do not invent parallel ones.
  2. Search the components before building one. There are six, plus two sheet formats; most needs are a link, a stamp, or a trail.
  3. No ad-hoc values: no raw hex, no one-off sizes, no new easing curve. The motion budget is two durations and one curve.
  4. Token names are roles. --trace is linework, --accent is the seal. Read what a token is for, not what colour it happens to be.
  5. One tenth, one accent. Content stays near a tenth of the surface. Seal, trace ripple or roundel - only one may lead, and there is never more than one seal.
  6. No weight, no fill, no depth. No bold, no panels or washes, no shadows, gradients or glass, and no radius except circles and the seal.

Why

The night theme is authored, not inverted. The trace colours are pulled down to roughly the same quietness against inkstone that they have against paper, and faint is set to the same 3.5 : 1 in both - so a ripple is exactly as loud at night as by day, and the contrast rule for faint text does not quietly change meaning when the theme flips.

04Form

The silhouette is a line, not a box. One-pixel hairlines, a shelf edge with angled ticks, and circles. Nothing has depth: no shadow, no gradient, no glass, and the only rounded shapes are true circles and the seal.

Why

A single line weight is what keeps a sparse page from looking unfinished. When every rule, edge and outline is the same 1px, the eye reads them as one drawing - add a 2px rule anywhere and it becomes the loudest thing on a page that has almost nothing else on it.

05Motion

Stillness. Two ambient moments - a ripple that slowly widens and fades under an object, and steam dots that drift up three pixels - and two short fades for interaction. Nothing slides, bounces, or scales on hover.

TokenValueWhere
--t-fade0.3sThe link's seal dot, chip border, input underline, pagination fill.
--t-ink0.35sThe stamp button inking. A touch slower, like ink taking.
--easeease-outThe only interaction curve.
--d-ripple7sOne ripple, scale 0.2 to 1, fading out - on --ease-ripple.
--ease-ripplecubic-bezier(0.16, 0.84, 0.44, 1)Fast release, long settle - how a ring on still water behaves.
--d-steam6sSteam dots drift 3px, ease-in-out, staggered 0.7s apart.
Why

Only opacity, colour and the two ambient drifts are allowed to move. A page this empty amplifies every motion on it, so anything faster or larger than a fade reads as an event. Under prefers-reduced-motion the ripple freezes at three-quarter size, the steam stops, and nothing is lost.

06Screen - still water

One optional surface treatment. Below the horizon the paper becomes still water seen at a low angle: hairline rings travel out from under the resting object. Move across the right-hand panel and the source follows you, slowly; press and a single ink ring drops into the surface.

Why

Enhancement only. Underneath the canvas is the complete static design - the ripple motif at the waterline - and the canvas stays transparent until its first frame, so a machine without WebGL2 loses nothing. It stops when scrolled off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion it draws one settled frame and never starts a loop. It draws lines and nothing else, so the emptiness rule holds inside the canvas too.

Where it belongs: the empty lower half of a sheet, under an object resting on a horizon. Not behind text, and never across a whole page - water with nothing resting on it is only noise.

Why

The objects are solid because everything else is a line. A 1px page needs a few points of real mass to rest the eye on, and a small filled silhouette carries that mass without a panel or a wash. The cutouts in each one are paper showing through - which is why, at night, they read as the ground rather than as highlights.

10Rules

The refusals matter more than the permissions.

Do
  • Keep every element small and let the paper carry the composition.
  • Anchor every object on a horizon, a trail or a ripple.
  • Let one accent lead each surface: a seal, a trace ripple, or a roundel.
  • Carry every essential word in --ink.
  • Check both themes with the masthead toggle - they are authored separately.
  • Set vertical words upright, whatever the script.
Do not
  • Fill any area larger than a silhouette. No panels, no washes.
  • Use a bold weight. Emphasis comes from placement and isolation.
  • Add shadows, gradients or glass.
  • Round a corner, except true circles and the seal.
  • Place more than one seal on a surface.
  • Let --trace, --trace-alt or --hairline carry text.
  • Run the still-water screen behind text or across a whole page.
