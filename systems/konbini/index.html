KONBINI

design system

one sky one scene whole pixels

コンビニ

A design system drawn as a series of pixel travel posters about quiet everyday places. Every surface is a poster: one vertical sky falling into snow-white paper, heavy display type floating in the sky, small type resting on the paper, and one carefully built pixel scene as the centrepiece.

Two things to hold on to. All illustration is pixel art on a whole-number grid - rectangles only, scaled 1x, 2x, 3x or 4x and never in between. And there is exactly one gradient: the vertical field behind the display type. Everything else is a flat fill.

00

read this first

  1. These values are the system. Every colour on this page is a token in css/konbini-core.css. Use them; do not add an eleventh ink.
  2. Look for an existing part before drawing a new one. The plate, the chip, the band and the folio cover more ground than they seem to.
  3. No ad-hoc values. No raw hex - pixel art included, it fills with the --art-* inks. No fractional pixel scale, no blur, no easing curve.
  4. Token names are roles. --lit is whatever catches light; --ink is every word on paper. Read what a token is for, not what colour it happens to be today.
  5. One gradient, vertical, and it is the field. Nothing else grades: not a button, not a word, not a shadow.
  6. The accent never carries text. It is the sign's colour - stripes, marks, a cone. A word set in it is a word nobody can read.

The old names were hues, and the night poster would have made every one of them a lie. What is navy by day is near-white at night; what is white on the field stays white, but the plate it used to share a name with goes dark. So --ink-strong names the job - the strongest ink on the paper - and --lit and --plate are separate roles that happen to match in daylight. --art-line is authored on its own for the same reason: an outline dark enough to sit beside text is invisible around a silhouette against a night sky.

Lit type is for the field only, and only at poster size. Against the daytime sky white sits at barely 2:1 - acceptable for an 8rem word that reads as a shape, useless for a sentence. So anything small on the field is set in ink-strong, and the display voice never carries body copy. Captions are lowercase for the same reason the headline is huge: the poster has one voice that shouts, and everything else speaks quietly underneath it.

The scene steps; it never scales. A 176 x 104 grid at 2.7px per pixel would put some pixel edges on half-pixels, and the browser would smear them. So the scene jumps between whole sizes at breakpoints and lets the margin absorb the difference. The same logic runs the whitespace: the poster is allowed to look emptier on a wide screen, because emptiness is the point.
03

form

the pixel rule

Everything is square and drawn in 2px steps. There is no radius, no blur and no stroke: a border is four flat edges that leave the corners empty, and a shadow is a second flat plate offset down and to the right.

The press lands exactly on the shadow, and that is the whole trick. Rest is 4px of offset, press moves the plate 4px - so pressing a button does not animate anything, it just shows you where the plate was always going to go. Every distance in the component set is a multiple of the same 2px step, which is what lets states snap without looking broken: a jump of one whole step reads as pixel art, a jump of 3.4px reads as a bug.
04

motion

posters do not move

Almost none. Two authored moments live in the hero scene - birds on the wire and one drifting cloud - and both move in whole steps with steps() timing. Component states have no transition at all: they snap.

momenttimingwhat moves
k-hop-a9s steps(1, end) infiniteFirst bird: shuffles 3px along the wire and hops 2px.
k-hop-b13s steps(1, end) infinite, 2s delaySecond bird: one hop back along the wire.
k-away17s steps(1, end) infinite, 5s delayThird bird: leaves for a moment and comes back.
k-drift160s steps(28) infinite alternateThe cloud: 28 grid pixels, one pixel per step, there and back.
statesnoneHover, press, focus and selection change in a single frame.

Hover this plate and press it: there is no in-between frame to see.

Steps, not curves, because a pixel cannot be half-way anywhere. An eased tween would move a bird through fractional positions and anti-alias it into a smudge for the length of the tween. steps() keeps every frame on the grid. Under prefers-reduced-motion the birds and the cloud stop where they are, and the poster is still complete - nothing on it ever depended on moving.
05

screen

enhancement only

One optional surface treatment: the pixel sky. It redraws the field as pixel art - cut into whole cells, its three stops dithered into each other, snow falling through it one cell at a time. The pointer is a lamp: it lifts the sky toward the horizon tone underneath it and pushes the snow sideways as wind.

It can only emit four colours, and they are all tokens. Every pixel is a choice between two field stops, or snow - never a blend - so the shader is structurally incapable of anti-aliasing, grading, or inventing a hue. That is the pixel rule and the one-gradient rule made mechanical. And it is enhancement only: the canvas is transparent until its first frame, so a machine without WebGL2 sees the plain field. It stops when scrolled off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion it draws one settled frame and never starts a loop.

Where it belongs: in a field block behind display type, or in an empty poster frame. Not behind body text, not in the hero scene - the scene already has its two authored moments - and never more than once on a page.

Every state is a change of plate, never a change of shape. A button does not grow, round off or glow when hovered; it moves one step and its shadow follows. A chip does not gain a border when selected; it inverts. Because nothing is added, nothing has to be animated in - which is what lets the whole set snap and still feel deliberate.

12

Every motif is a quiet thing at rest. A scene with nobody in it, a stripe, a page number. The system has no badges, no counters, no alerts in its vocabulary, and adding one would change what it is: a poster that shouts is an advertisement. Before adding an eighth motif, ask whether it would still belong on a poster nobody is being sold anything from.

09

rules

the refusals matter more

do

  • Keep margins enormous and elements few: one centrepiece per poster.
  • Snap everything to the grid: 2px steps in CSS, whole-number scale for pixel art.
  • Fill illustration with the art inks, so a scene follows the page to night.
  • Set small type on the field in ink-strong; keep lit for poster-size display.
  • Author both posters and look at each with the masthead toggle.
  • Let states snap. A plate moves one step or it does not move.

do not

  • Draw curves, circles or strokes in illustration. Rects only, integer coordinates.
  • Add a second gradient. No gradient text, no gradient buttons, no radial anything.
  • Use a blurred shadow, ever. Shadows are flat offset plates.
  • Put text on the accent. It is stripes, cones and marks.
  • Set body text in recede. Recede is for distance and for disabled.
  • Scale pixel art by a fraction: 1.5x is not a size.
  • Animate anything but the hero birds and cloud - and the optional pixel sky, once, never behind text.
