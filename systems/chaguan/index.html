Chaguan · Ink and Paper System

Plain, task-first interface dressed in literati ink-painting clothes: brush display type, solid-ink still lifes over a flat disc, and a vermilion seal as punctuation - all on warm paper pinned to a mount.

Two things to hold on to. The decoration never enters the working surface: brush art lives in posters and empty states, and every control is plain sans with a 2px line icon. And the seal is the only hot colour - it is punctuation, so it appears at most twice on a surface and never carries a sentence.

Read this first

  1. These values are the system. Every token below exists in css/chaguan-core.css. Use them; do not invent parallel ones - no raw hex, no new radius, no new duration.
  2. Two registers, never mixed. Brush type and ink art belong to posters and empty states. Anything a thumb operates gets the UI sans and a 2px line icon.
  3. The seal is punctuation. At most two per surface, never behind body copy; its only other job is the live count badge.
  4. The disc never carries text. It sits behind art or a title and holds nothing of its own.
  5. Token names are roles. --primary is a deep brown fill by day and a pale tan fill after dark; --sheet is the raised surface, not "white". Check both with the toggle.
  6. Search before you build. A row, a tile, a round button and a pill cover almost every control; a sixth shape is a conversation about the system.

After dark, the fill and its text trade places - which is why the tokens are named for roles. By day --primary is a deep brown with cream on it; by lamplight a deep fill would vanish into the mount, so it becomes a pale tan carrying dark text. The same flip turns the disc from a noon sun into a dim moon behind pale ink. A token called "coffee" or "white" would be lying in one theme or the other.

Legibility is never asked of the brush. A calligraphic face is beautiful exactly because its strokes vary, and that variation is what makes it slow to read at small sizes. So the brush only ever sets a mood at 1rem and up, and every number, count and state belongs to the UI sans. If a piece of text has to be read correctly, it is not brush.

Section 04 · Soft blocks, square seals

The seal is square because everything else is not. In a field of soft blocks and pills, a 2px corner is the loudest shape available, and the seal needs to be loud at 30px. Round the seal to match its neighbours and it becomes a red dot - a notification, not a signature.

Motion

Section 05 · A press and a scroll

Restrained. A press darkens and settles 1px; the notice line scrolls on a slow loop. Nothing else moves. Press the button and hover the bar.

A press is a stamp, not a bounce. 100ms and one pixel is the feeling of a seal meeting paper: short, final, no overshoot. Under prefers-reduced-motion the transitions drop out and the notice line stands still, and nothing on the page is lost.

Section 06 · Ink wash

One optional surface treatment: an ink wash. Three ridges of ink drift slowly across absorbent paper in front of the disc, each darkest at its crest and thinning to mist below. The pointer is a loaded brush - rest it on the right-hand panel and ink blooms into the fibres.

UniformValueWhat it does
uReach0.2Radius of the brush bloom, in stage heights.
uBleed0.6How far ink feathers past a hard edge - the ridge crests and the frayed rim of the bloom. At 0 the wash turns into cut paper.
uFibre0.04Paper fibre, drawn as the faintest possible ink so it stays inside the palette.
uDrift0.018Ridge drift, in stage widths per second. The near ridge moves fastest, which is the only depth cue.
uDiscAt / uDiscR0.70, 0.62 / 0.17Where the disc sits and how large. The CSS fallback draws it in the same place.
uPaper / uDisc / uInktokensRead from --paper, --disc and --ink at runtime, and re-read on every theme change.
springk 40 / d 8The brush follows the pointer on a spring, heavier than a cursor, so it arrives rather than snaps.
The palette rule is structural here. The shader's last line is mix(mix(uPaper, uDisc, disc), uInk, ink) - only three pigments go in, so only three can come out, and the greys between them. After dark the same line gives pale ink on dark paper in front of a dim moon, with no second shader. It stops rendering off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion it draws one settled frame and never starts a loop. Without WebGL2 the canvas never becomes visible, and the left panel is the whole design.

Where it belongs: behind a poster, or filling an empty state that has nothing else to show. Not behind a list, a field or any running text - it is the decorative register, and the working surface never carries it.

Alternate ground - .cg-alt

One class swaps the grounds: --stage, --paper, --card and --sheet take their --alt-* values, type on the ground takes --on-alt. Same grammar everywhere else.

Every control is one of four shapes - a soft tile, a pill, a round button, a row. That is deliberate: the posters above them are where the variety lives, so the working surface can afford to be boring, and a reader who has seen one pill knows every pill.

30px or 44px, 2px corner, a carved mark or a two-letter monogram in --on-seal. The caption rule labels a poster the way a colophon labels a scroll.

The disc is what lets flat ink read as a painting. A solid-black teapot on cream is clip art; put a flat disc behind it and the eye reads sun, space and a horizon that is not drawn. That is why the disc may be halved, washed or pushed off the edge, but never removed - and why it never carries text, which would turn it back into a button.

Rules

Section 10 · The refusals matter more

Do

  • Keep brush art in posters and empty states. Give task rows the 2px line icons.
  • Put the disc behind art or a title - full, washed, halved or off the edge.
  • Keep values, counts and states in the UI sans.
  • Give each surface one deep block that owns its decision.
  • Set small text on the mount in --ink only; --primary there is for large titles.
  • Check both themes with the masthead toggle before calling anything done.

Do not

  • Use brush illustrations as icons inside rows, tiles or tabs.
  • Put text on the disc, ever.
  • Use more than two seals on a surface - a seal is punctuation, not a pattern.
  • Set task-critical text in brush or caption.
  • Add a border or a third shadow; blocks separate by fill.
  • Use the alternate ground on more than one surface of a composition - it is a variant, not a second theme.
  • Run the ink wash behind a list or running text.
