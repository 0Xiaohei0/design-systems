A monochrome arcade targeting-HUD. Two grounds, one visual world, and no chroma anywhere.

Two things to hold on to. Emphasis is a filled block, never a colour - there is no accent hue to reach for. And status is encoded in form: filled is active, outline is idle, dashed is disabled or in flux. Read shapes, not hues.

The system is SDPG-HUD

Read this first

  1. These values are the system. Everything below is a token that exists in css/hud-core.css. Use them; do not invent parallel ones.
  2. Never add colour. The moment a hue appears, the whole encoding collapses - because form is carrying the meaning that colour usually carries, a single red breaks every other signal on the screen.
  3. Reach for a filled block before anything else. Emphasis, selection and active state are all one move: invert the surface.
  4. Both grounds, always. Blueprint and CRT are hand-authored, and nothing warns you if you only checked one. Hit Invert.
  5. Names are roles. --ink is the foreground, not the colour black - on the CRT ground it is near-white.
  6. No ad-hoc values. No raw hex, no one-off sizes, no new easing. If a token does not cover your case, that is a conversation about the system.

§05

Motion

One loop

Almost nothing moves, and what does moves instantly. There is no easing curve in this system - transitions are linear and short enough that a curve would not be visible anyway.

TokenValueWhere
tint120msBackground and colour on buttons and icon buttons.
track150msThe toggle knob travelling, and its track filling.
press50msThe 1px downward nudge on a pressed button.
blink1.4s steps(1)The live indicator. Stepped, never faded - a CRT dot is on or off.
steps(1) is the whole motion philosophy in one value. A smooth fade would read as a modern web animation and break the register instantly. Everything that changes here snaps, because the hardware this system imitates could not interpolate.

prefers-reduced-motion kills the blink and every transition outright - there is nothing in the system whose meaning depends on movement.

§06

Screen

Enhancement only

One optional surface treatment: a phosphor sweep. A beam rotates and decays across the screen, scanlines cut it, grain sits on top, and range rings lock to the pointer. The pointer is the target - move it across the right-hand panel and the reticle follows on a spring.

Static fallback · grid and scanline

UniformValueWhat it does
uSweep0.55 rad/sBeam rate. One revolution takes about eleven seconds, slow enough to read as instrumentation rather than as a spinner.
uPersist2.3Trail decay. Higher is a shorter tail - this is the phosphor, and it is the only thing that makes the sweep read as a sweep.
uScan0.14Scanline depth, at one cycle every two device pixels.
uGrain0.05Phosphor grain. Enough to kill banding, not enough to read as noise.
uRings / uRingGap3 / 0.075Range rings around the target, breathing on a 1.6s pulse.
uGround / uInktokensRead from the live values, so the treatment inverts with the ground like everything else.
springk 52 / d 9The reticle is followed on a spring, so it has mass and settles instead of snapping.
The no-chroma rule is structural here, not a matter of care. The shader accumulates a single intensity and its last line is mix(uGround, uInk, ink) - so the only colours it is capable of emitting are the two ground tokens and the greys between them. There is no way to leak a hue into this panel without rewriting that line, which is a far better guarantee than remembering not to.

Where it belongs: behind a hero, or filling an empty state or a panel that has nothing in it yet. Not behind a working surface - it is the decorative register, and it lives under the same quarantine as the gauges and the D-pad. Under prefers-reduced-motion it renders one settled frame and never starts the loop; with no WebGL2 the static panel on the left is the whole design.

Beyond the icons and the character, four pieces of furniture carry the register: the corner bracket on panels, the registration cross, the scanline wash, and the viewport frame pinned to the four corners of the window. §10

Rules

The refusals matter most
Do
  • Encode state in form: filled is active, outline is idle, dashed is disabled or in flux.
  • Reach for the inverted block for emphasis and selection. It is the whole vocabulary.
  • Author and check both grounds. Blueprint and CRT are hand-written, not derived.
  • Keep mono as the default and let the body face appear only in running prose.
  • Let the dashed edge mean something. It is a state, and spending it as decoration costs you a signal.
Do not
  • Add any hue. Not for errors, not for success, not "just this once" - form is already carrying that load.
  • Add a shadow. There is no elevation model; on the CRT ground a shadow is a grey smear.
  • Let the decorative register - gauges, D-pad, scanlines - onto a working surface.
  • Nest panels. Two hairlines a few pixels apart read as a rendering fault.
  • Give anything a radius above 4px, pills excepted. Round corners drain the instrument out of it.
  • Interpolate. The live dot steps; it never fades.
  • Run the phosphor sweep behind a working surface. It is decorative register, under the same quarantine as the gauges.
