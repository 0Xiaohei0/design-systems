Design System

新聞SHIMBUN

A newspaper desk as an interface: warm paper ground, one vermilion spot colour, oversized tight headlines, hairline rules, and mono annotations. Type does the talking - the only decoration left is a rule, a kicker, and one scrim.

Two things to hold on to. Colour names are roles, not hues: in the dark theme --ink is a warm off-white and --bg is a warm near-black, and both names stay true. And vermilion is the voice, teal is the tool - vermilion exists to make a reader look up, teal marks what can be operated. Neither is a decorative colour.

12ROLE TOKENS

Read this first

  1. These values are the system. Every number below exists in css/tokens.css. Use them; do not invent parallel ones.
  2. There are exactly two saturated colours - vermilion and teal. Adding a third is the same as announcing that vermilion no longer means anything.
  3. Radius is capped at 6px, pills excepted. Print is not round. This is the system's skeleton, not a preference.
  4. Both papers are hand-authored. Dark is not derived from light, and nothing will warn you if you only checked one. Use the toggle.
  5. Shadow is scarce. Only the dialog and the card hover can afford one. Everything else lies flat on the paper.
  6. Do not invent an easing curve. Two durations and one ease are the whole motion budget.

Sections are separated by the 2px rule, not by whitespace. So the gaps between them can sit tighter than instinct suggests. In this language whitespace is paper and the heavy rule is the paragraph break - take the rules away and add air to compensate, and the page immediately falls apart into a pile of loose cards.

Refusals

  • No gradients - except the scrim under an image, which exists only so text on it stays readable.
  • No shadows - except the dialog, and the card's hover lift.
  • No pure black or white on the light theme. Every neutral is warm.
  • No radius above 6px, pills excepted. This system does not do cute.
  • No icon or illustration where a rule and a kicker would do.
Two of the radius steps are only 2px apart, and that is deliberate. 4px and 6px are nearly indistinguishable; what is distinguishable is the gulf between them and the 999px pill. The system needs exactly two attitudes - square and round - and none of the lukewarm stops in between.

Section 05Motion

2 durations

Quiet. Two durations, one curve, and only transform, opacity and colour are allowed to move.

TokenValueWhere
--t-hover0.18sCard lift, pill inversion, button fill. Seen constantly, so kept short.
--t-zoom0.35sDialog entrance. The one moment allowed to slow down.
--easeeaseThe only curve in the system. Do not introduce a second.
lifttranslateY(-3px)Card hover. The largest displacement anywhere in the language.
3px is a ceiling, not a starting point. Printed matter does not levitate; 3px is already the maximum concession this system makes to the paper metaphor. Any more and the card stops being a sheet and becomes a widget, taking the credibility of the whole material with it. prefers-reduced-motion zeroes all of it and the page is still complete.

Section 06Live ink

enhancement only

One optional surface treatment: a halftone press that resolves the paper into printed dots and lets the pointer press vermilion ink into it. Move across the right-hand panel - the dots swell under the cursor the way ink spreads where a plate bites hardest.

Static fallback - flat paper
Live - move the pointer across it
UniformValueWhat it does
uCell7.0pxHalftone pitch, in device pixels. Below about 5 the rosette turns into noise on a low-density screen.
uAngle15°Screen angle. The traditional single-colour angle, which is what stops the grid reading as pixels.
uRadius0.34Reach of the pointer, in screen widths.
uBite0.55How much larger a dot grows at the centre of the press.
uInk--accentRead from the live token, so the ink follows the theme and never hardcodes a hex.
springk 58 / d 9The pointer is followed on a spring, so the press has weight and settles instead of snapping.
Enhancement only, and it must stay that way. What sits underneath is the complete static design - the canvas is transparent until its first frame, so a machine without WebGL2 loses nothing at all. It stops rendering when scrolled off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion it draws a single settled frame and never starts a loop. The ink colour is read from --accent at runtime rather than written into the shader, which is the only reason it survives a theme flip.

Where it belongs: behind a masthead, or filling a media frame that has no image yet. Not behind body text - a halftone under running type costs you the contrast the colour section just spent its whole budget defending.

Section 10Rules

the refusals matter more

Do

  • Let vermilion do one job: make a reader look up. Kickers and one accent tag, once or twice a screen.
  • Let teal mean operable: focus rings and note plates. It is the tool colour, not a second decorative hue.
  • Break sections on the 2px rule and rows on the hairline. The two weights have separate jobs.
  • Hand-author both papers, and actually look at each one with the masthead toggle.
  • Give every piece of meta information to mono: numbers, counts, tags, dates.
  • Keep the neutrals warm. A cold grey on this paper looks like dirt.

Do not

  • Add a third saturated colour. Doing so announces that vermilion is no longer special.
  • Add a gradient - except the scrim that keeps text on an image readable.
  • Add a shadow - except the dialog, and the card's 3px hover.
  • Use pure black or white as a light-theme ground.
  • Open a radius past 6px, pills excepted.
  • Name a token after a hue. One theme flip and the name is a lie.
  • Run the halftone under body text. It is a masthead and empty-frame treatment, nothing more.
