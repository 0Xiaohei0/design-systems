04/004 BASE STOCK

Every element is a printed label.

OBJEKT is a brutalist industrial label system. Buttons are stamps, values are serials, panels are paper stock, and the page is a concrete sheet inside a black press frame. Print, not screen.

Two things to hold on to. Colour is material: four stocks and their inks are the whole palette, and there is no accent hue to reach for. And depth is a hard offset shadow: a sticker lifts off the sheet with zero blur, or it lies flat. Nothing in between.

Read this first

Handling instructions / 6 items
  1. These values are the system. Every token below exists in css/objekt-core.css. Use them; do not invent parallel ones.
  2. Pick a stock before you pick a colour. A surface is lead, mark, plate or base, and the stock class sets its ink. Never set text colour on a label by hand.
  3. No radius, no blur, no gradient, no opacity. Disabled is a pre-mixed solid ink, not a faded one. These four refusals are the silhouette.
  4. Search for an existing label before building a second one. The strip, the plate, the stamp and the serial plate cover almost everything.
  5. Token names are roles. --stock-lead is the loudest label, not the colour black - on the night sheet it is the palest thing on the page.
  6. Both sheets, always. Day and night are hand-authored and nothing warns you if you only checked one. Flip the sheet.

Why Secondary text is always a step of the stock's own ink, never a mid grey. A mid grey on mark or plate stock sits at the same luminance as the paper and disappears; --ink-2 is a darker step of the ink itself, so it clears 4.5:1 on every stock on both sheets. And disabled is a pre-mixed solid rather than an opacity, because a translucent ink would pick up whatever stock it sits on and read as a different colour on each one.

strip rest6px 6px 0 --shade

--lift4px 4px 0 --frame
A sticker peeled up: translate(-2px, -2px) plus this.
radius0
Everywhere, including inputs.
Why Two shadow colours, two meanings. --shade is a label lying on the sheet: soft in tone, never in edge. --frame is a label peeled up toward you, so its shadow is the darkest thing on the page. Keep them apart and a reader can tell resting from lifted without being told; blur either one and the page stops being paper.

Motion

SEC.05 / 10 - ONE MOMENT

Print does not move. The system has exactly one authored motion - the sticker lift - and everything else changes state instantly. Hover the stamp below, then press it.

Live - hover, press, tab to it

--t-lift120msThe lift, on transform and box-shadow only.
--ease-liftcubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.2, 1)Fast out, hard landing. The only curve.
hovertranslate(-2px, -2px)Plus --lift. The largest movement in the system.
activetranslate(0, 0)Pressed flat, shadow gone.
checkboxinstantThe X is stamped, not drawn.
Why A stamp lands, it does not glide. The curve front-loads almost all of the movement, so the lift feels like a thumb peeling a sticker and the press feels like it hitting the sheet. Anything slower reads as a screen animation. Under prefers-reduced-motion the transition is removed and states still change - nothing here depends on movement to be understood.

Screen

SEC.06 / 10 - ENHANCEMENT ONLY

One optional surface treatment: a thermal head. A sheet of die-cut labels feeds slowly out of the printer, already carrying their index block, data rows and barcode. The pointer is the print head - move it across the right-hand panel and the stock burns in square dots under it. Hold still and it burns solid. What it printed rides up with the paper and cools off.

Static fallback - unprinted die-cut sheet
Live - move the pointer across it
UniformValueWhat it does
uDot4px x DPRThermal dot pitch. Each dot is burnt or not as a whole, with a hairline gap between dots as a real head leaves.
uLabel34 x 22 dotsLabel pitch, so a label is 136 x 88px. The static fallback uses the same pitch.
uFeedPx3 dots/sPaper feed. Slow enough to read as a machine idling, not as scrolling.
uRadius0.075Head reach, in stage heights. Squeezed 2.2x along the feed, because a head is a line across the paper.
uTrail32 x 45msHead samples, stored in sheet space so the print moves with the paper. Each one carries heat 0.34, cooling on a 0.9s time constant.
dither4 x 4 orderedHeat becomes density of burnt dots. There is no grey on a thermal head, so there is none here.
uStock / uInk / uCuttokens--stock-base, --ink and --ink-dis, read live and re-read when the sheet flips.
springk 60 / d 10The head follows the pointer on a spring, so it has the weight of a carriage and settles instead of snapping.
Why The print rules are structural here, not a matter of care. Every pixel the shader writes is one of exactly three token colours - stock, ink or die line - because ink is decided per dot by a hard threshold and never blended. It cannot emit a gradient, a blur or a hue without rewriting its last two lines. It is also enhancement only: the canvas is transparent until its first frame, so a machine without WebGL2 sees the complete static sheet on the left. It stops when scrolled off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion it draws one settled frame and never starts a loop.

Where it belongs: an empty state, a loading or printing moment, or a hero with nothing else in it. Not behind body text or a form - the printed dots sit at full ink density and would take the contrast the colour section just defended.

Rules

SEC.10 / 10 - THE REFUSALS MATTER MORE
Pass
  • Use hard edges and hard offset shadows. Zero blur, always.
  • Pick a stock class and let it set the ink, secondary ink and disabled ink.
  • Spend lead stock sparingly: section strips, the primary stamp, error stamps.
  • Draw every mark in currentColor so it follows the stock and the sheet.
  • Mark disabled with a dashed edge as well as a lighter ink.
  • Check both sheets with the masthead flip before shipping anything.
Rejected
  • No gradients, no border-radius, no blur, no soft shadows.
  • No transparency or opacity effects. Disabled inks are pre-mixed solids.
  • No accent hue, not even for errors. Errors are stamps.
  • No mid-grey text on mark or plate stock. Use the stock's own --ink-2.
  • No token named for a hue or material. One sheet flip and the name is a lie.
  • No glows, glass, rounded chrome or decorative motion. And no thermal head behind body text.
