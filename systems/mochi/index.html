MOCHI 麻薯

A soft neumorphic widget system. Every surface is pressed from one warm material, the same colour as the page, and depth comes from light - never from lines.

Two things to hold on to. The material has exactly two states: raised at rest, pressed when active. And colour sits inside the material, never instead of it - pastels are plates laid into a pillow, not pillows of their own.

Read this first

  1. These values are the system. Every token below exists in css/mochi-core.css. Use them; do not invent parallel ones.
  2. Look for an existing part before moulding a new one. A widget, a plate and a tray cover almost every container you will need.
  3. No ad-hoc values. No raw hex, no new shadow, no new easing. The two shadow recipes are written once, in terms of --light and --shade; a theme changes the lights, never the recipe.
  4. Token names are roles. --cue means "look here", --active means "switched on". Read what a token is for, not what hue it happens to be.
  5. No borders, ever. The focus ring is the only outline in the system. If you need a line, you need a plate or a well instead.
  6. Light comes from the top-left, always. Flip one shadow and the whole page stops reading as one lit object.

Every fill role comes in three strengths because pastel is a bad place to put text. A plain pastel sits at about 3:1 under either ink - lovely as a fill, illegible as a label. So a role that needs to carry words gets a deep plate for large bold light text and a tint plate for small dark text, and the plain fill is kept for colour alone. The deep plates were darkened until light text cleared 3:1; the rule was always "deepen the plate, never shrink the text".

A raised or pressed surface is always --ground - the same colour as the page. That is what makes it look moulded out of the page rather than placed on it. The moment a fill differs, it is no longer the material; it is a plate, and a plate lies flat inside a widget with a small shadow. A pastel button with a big raised shadow is two ideas fighting.
Section 05 · soft and small

Motion

Motion is the material being pressed, and nothing else. Two durations, one curve, and one blink.

TokenValueWhere
--press-ease120ms ease-outRaised to pressed on box-shadow, fill changes, the toggle knob, tooltips. Fast enough to feel like a fingertip.
--fade-ease240ms ease-outThe face-plate blush and the screen canvas fading in. Things that appear rather than things that are pushed.
mochi-blink1s steps(1)The flip-digit colon drops to 15% opacity for half of each second. A tick, not a fade.
reduced motion0.01msTransitions collapse, the colon stays lit, the blush stays visible, the screen draws one still frame.
Nothing floats. There is no hover lift, no bounce, no scale-up: a soft object that rises toward the cursor stops being a pillow and becomes a balloon. The only change of depth is the one a finger causes - raised to pressed, and back.
Section 06 · enhancement only

Screen

One optional surface treatment: a dough press. A single pillow of ground material, lit from the top-left like everything else, breathing very slightly. Rest the pointer on the right-hand panel and a fingertip touches it; hold the button down and the dough gives, swells around the dimple, and blushes where it is pressed deepest.

Static fallback - one raised pillow in CSS
Live - rest the pointer on it, then hold to press

Uniforms

UniformValueWhat it does
uSoft0.11Width of the pillow's shoulder, in panel heights. The shoulder is convex - steep at the ground, rounding over at the top - so it reads as a pillow; narrower and it reads as a bevel.
uRelief0.055How steep the height field reads to the light. Higher looks harder, not softer.
uReach0.13Radius of the fingertip, in panel heights.
uDepth1.8Dimple depth at full press, in pillow heights. A ring swells around it by 22% of that.

uBreath0.035Amplitude of the slow idle swell. Zero under reduced motion.
uBlush0.25Ceiling on how far the deepest point warms toward --cue.
uGround / uLight / uShade / uCuetokensRead from the live values and re-read on every theme change, so the pillow is always the page's own material.
springk 42 / d 8.5Pointer, touch and press all follow springs, so the dough gives and recovers instead of snapping.
The shader is the material rule, drawn instead of box-shadowed. Its light is fixed at the top-left, a flat surface renders as exactly --ground, and every other pixel is the ground mixed toward --light or --shade by how far it tilts - the same two colours the CSS recipes use. Only the blush borrows --cue, as the face plate does. It is enhancement only: the canvas is transparent until its first frame, so the CSS pillow underneath is the whole design on a machine without WebGL2. It stops when scrolled off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion it draws one settled frame and never starts a loop.

Where it belongs: an empty state, a hero, or a panel that has nothing in it yet - somewhere a reader might idly poke. Not behind text or controls: a surface that dimples under the pointer competes with every button placed on it, and the buttons should win.

Hover changes nothing but the cursor. In a system with only two depths, a hover state would need a third, and the third depth is where neumorphism usually goes muddy. Focus is different - keyboard users cannot see a cursor - so focus gets the one outline the system allows: 2px of --focus, 3px out.

Section 10 · the refusals matter more

Rules

The same content, twice: once moulded from the material, once built the usual way.

Specimen card

Depth from light. Same colour as the page.

Do

Specimen card

Border, hard shadow, cold white.

Do not borders, hard edges, cold neutrals
Do
  • Mould everything from --ground. Depth comes from light.
  • Keep the light top-left on every shadow, in both themes.
  • Put colour on plates inside widgets, with the small plate shadow.
  • Use a -tint plate for small text on colour and a -deep plate for large bold light text.
  • Spend --cue in small doses - a dot, a colon, a blush.
  • Keep every neutral warm, in the dark theme too.
Do not
  • Draw a border or outline anywhere - the focus ring is the only exception.
  • Use pure black, pure white or a cold grey.
  • Use a hard shadow, or write a new shadow instead of reusing the recipes.
  • Add a second deep plate colour. --anchor only, used sparingly.
  • Put small light text on any pastel, or small dark text on a plain fill.
  • Add a third depth - no hover lift, no floating.
  • Run the dough press behind text or controls.
