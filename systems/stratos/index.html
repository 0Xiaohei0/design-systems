STRATOS

SIGNAL_OVERLAY_KIT
KIT.V0.1 // TWO GROUNDS ACTIVE

A signal-overlay kit: every element is a self-contained instrument readout that could be composited over footage and still hold. Dense mono labels, heavy grotesque numerals, segmented bars, corner brackets and hazard stripes - drawn in one ink and one signal hue.

Two things to hold on to. There is exactly one hue, and it means live - the signal colour marks what is active, selected, focused or alarming, never what is merely decorative. And every mark is flat: no glow, no blur, no radius, no gradient fade. A readout either holds or flips.

READ_THIS_FIRSTCONTRACT FOR WHOEVER IMPLEMENTS

  1. These values are the system. Every token below exists in css/stratos-core.css. Use them; do not invent parallel ones.
  2. Search the marks before drawing a new one. Chip, stripe, segmented bar, readout, bracket, cross, schematic, feed row - a new element is almost always one of these eight.
  3. No ad-hoc values. No raw hex, no one-off sizes, no easing curve. Motion is steps() or linear, nothing else.
  4. Token names are roles. --ink is the foreground, near-white on black glass and near-black in daylight. --signal is the live hue, not "the orange".
  5. The signal hue is spent on live things only. Active, selected, focused, alarming. Put it on an idle element and every live element on the screen loses its meaning. The hazard stripe and the wordmark square are the only standing exceptions, which is why both are rationed.
  6. Nothing is soft. No glow, no blur, no radius, no shadow, no gradient fade. The hazard stripe's gradient is hard-stop strips, and it is the only one.
SEC.01

Colour

8 ROLE TOKENS

Eight role tokens: two grounds, two inks, one signal, one hairline, and the two inks that sit on a filled block. Every swatch shows black glass on the left and daylight glass on the right, so both themes are visible at once - and the FLIP_THEME button in the masthead flips every other specimen on the page.

Black glass is the default, and it sits on bare :root. This is an overlay language - it is designed to be composited over footage, and its natural ground is the dark glass in front of it. Daylight glass is a hand-authored second ground for pages that live on a light screen: an OS light preference reaches it, and an explicit data-theme wins in either direction. It is not a derived inversion. The two neutrals swap, but the signal hue is re-cut from #FF6A00 to #B84300, because the bright cut falls to 2.6:1 on a light ground and the deeper one keeps signal text above 4.5:1. The same move flips --on-signal from near-black to near-white.

The display closes in and the mono opens up, and that is the whole hierarchy. Tight heavy numerals read as instruments; small tracked mono reads as annotation. The underscore grammar does the rest: a label with no spaces reads as an identifier, never as a sentence.
SEC.03

Space and layout

DENSE

The odd numbers are not a mistake. 3, 7 and 9 exist because the marks are built on a pixel grid, not a spacing scale: a 9px cell with a 3px gap is a 12px pitch, and a 9px cross needs its 1px stroke on pixel 4 to sit dead centre. Round these to a 4px scale and every segmented bar and cross goes soft by half a pixel.
SEC.04

Form

0PX RADIUS

The silhouette is a 1px line and a square corner. There is no radius anywhere, no shadow, and no elevation beyond one flat fill - a raised block is --panel, and that is the whole depth model.

Flat is what makes it composite. A glow or a soft shadow is drawn assuming a known background; laid over footage it turns into a grey smear. A 1px line and a solid block survive any backdrop, which is the only thing an overlay has to do.
SEC.05

Motion

2 AUTHORED MOMENTS

Instruments, not decoration. Two authored moments - a stepped blink on live things, and one flat scan column - and every other state change is an instant flip with no transition at all.

LIVE

TokenValueWhere
--t-blink1.4s steps(1, end)Live status dots and the block caret. On or off, never a fade.
--t-scan8s linearOne flat column crosses a hero frame in the first 32% of the cycle, then dwells off-frame.
presstranslateY(1px)Buttons on :active. The largest displacement in the system.
hoverinstantFill and edge flip in the same frame. There is no easing curve to reuse.
A readout either holds or flips. An eased transition implies the value passed through the states in between, which an instrument never claims. Stepped timing keeps motion honest, and the scan spends most of its cycle off-frame so it reads as a periodic check rather than a loop to watch. Under prefers-reduced-motion the blink stops, the scan is removed, and nothing is lost.
SEC.06

Screen

ENHANCEMENT ONLY

One optional surface treatment: interference contours. Two slow emitters ring outward across the glass and their interference is drawn as hairline contours. The pointer is a third emitter - move it across the right-hand panel and, inside a bracketed reach, the field is read out as segmented-bar cells: signal where it peaks, outline where it does not.

The flat-marks rule is structural here, not a matter of care. The shader composites exactly four things - --ground, the hairline mix of --ink, --ink, and --signal - and every readout cell samples the field once, so it can only be fully lit or dark. There is no path for a glow or a fade to reach the screen. The signal hue appears only while the pointer is present, so at rest the panel obeys the same live-only rule as everything else. The canvas is transparent until its first frame, stops rendering when scrolled off screen, and under prefers-reduced-motion draws one settled frame and never starts a loop.

Where it belongs: an empty hero frame, or a schematic slot that has nothing to show yet. Not behind readouts or body text - contours under a feed row compete with the dotted leaders and cost the dim ink its contrast.

SEC.10

Rules

THE REFUSALS MATTER MORE

DO

  • Spend the signal hue on live things only: active, selected, focused, alarming.
  • Keep every mark flat: 1px lines, solid blocks, square corners.
  • Keep text at --ink-dim or brighter. There is no darker grey.
  • Use the naming grammar everywhere, specimen data included: underscores, WORD.DIGIT, values with a unit suffix.
  • Change one property per state, and flip it instantly.
  • Check both grounds with the toggle. Daylight is hand-authored, not derived.

DO_NOT

  • Add a second hue. The moment there are two, neither means live.
  • Add glow, blur, shadow, radius or a gradient fade. The hazard stripe is hard-stop strips and the only gradient.
  • Set signal text below 14px bold, on either ground.
  • Ease a state change, or animate anything for flavour.
  • Name a token after its hue. On daylight glass the signal is a different cut and the ink is black.
  • Run the interference contours behind readouts. It is an empty-frame treatment, nothing more.
